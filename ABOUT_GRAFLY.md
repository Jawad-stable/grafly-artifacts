## 1. The Big Idea

Most people learn design the wrong way. They watch tutorials, scroll through
inspiration boards, and read about typography — but they never actually move
a single pixel. Watching is not doing.

**Grafly flips that.** It's a phone-sized design school where every lesson is
a tiny, hands-on challenge. Tap the better headline. Drag the elements into a
balanced layout. Spot the broken alignment. Pick which of two posters works.
Every minute of "lesson" time is a minute of *deciding* like a designer would.

The whole experience is wrapped in the friendly habits of a great learning
game: short streaks, daily goals, a cute mascot, and a bright reward loop. So
you keep coming back, and a few weeks later you realise you can actually
*see* design — the gridlines, the rhythm, the moments where something feels
off — instead of just admiring it from the outside.

The tagline says it best: **"Learn design by actually designing."**

---

## 2. Who Grafly Is For

Grafly is built for three kinds of people:

- **Aspiring designers** who want a guided, structured way to build their eye
  before they start their first real portfolio project.
- **Students** who are studying anything visual (graphic design, product
  design, illustration, photography, fashion) and want a daily warm-up that
  reinforces the fundamentals.
- **Career-switchers** coming from another field who don't have time for a
  full bootcamp and need bite-sized, confidence-building practice that fits
  in their commute or lunch break.

If you've ever opened a design tool and felt frozen by the blank canvas,
Grafly is the warm-up you didn't know you needed.

## 3. What's in the Box

Grafly is a whole product family, not just one app. Here's what each piece
is and who it's for:

| Piece | What it is | Who sees it |
|---|---|---|
| **Grafly app** | The phone app where you actually take lessons, talk to the mentor, and earn streaks. | Every user. |
| **The brain behind the app** | A quiet helper that lives on the internet and powers the AI mentor and the spoken voice. You never see it directly — it just makes the magic happen. | Nobody — it works in the background. |
| **Brand reel** | A short, cinematic ~2-minute animated video that introduces Grafly: the mascot pops in, the features fly through, the tagline lands. Useful for app store previews, social posts, and demo days. | Marketing audiences and curious newcomers. |
| **Pitch deck** | The investor and hackathon presentation that explains the problem, the audience, and where Grafly is going next. | Investors, judges, partners. |
| **Design playground** | An internal sandbox where the team prototypes new screens and compares variants side by side before shipping them into the main app. | The team only. |

Together these five pieces cover the entire arc: build the product, explain
the product, prove the product, and keep improving it.

## 4. The Look and Feel

Grafly was deliberately designed to *not* look like every other learning app.
Instead of bouncy cartoon gradients and rainbow confetti, it borrows from
**editorial design** — the world of fashion magazines, art books, and
collage-style posters.

That means:

- **Big, confident headlines** the way a magazine cover sets the mood for the
  whole issue.
- **Generous white space** so each screen feels calm, not cluttered.
- **Near-black pill-shaped buttons** for the most important action on each
  screen — never neon, never shouty.
- **Subtle squiggle motifs** drifting in the background like the kind of
  stamp or watermark you'd see printed on a thick piece of art paper.
- **A soft, light default theme** with a full dark-mode option for night
  sessions.
- **Custom fonts** chosen specifically to make the app feel handcrafted,
  not generic.

The result is something that feels closer to a beautifully designed
notebook than a typical mobile game. People who care about design notice it
within the first three seconds — which is, of course, the whole point.

## 5. Your First Time Opening Grafly

The first time you launch Grafly, it walks you through a friendly
seven-step welcome tour. There's a clear "Skip for now" exit at every step
so you're never trapped, but if you stick with it the flow feels less like
a form and more like a conversation:

1. **Welcome** — A warm hello from the mascot and a gentle theme toggle so
   you can pick light or dark before anything else.
2. **Name** — What should we call you?
3. **Goal** — Why are you here? Improve your eye, change careers, prep for
   school, or just have fun?
4. **Level** — Where would you place yourself today: total beginner,
   getting comfortable, or already pretty solid?
5. **Time** — How many minutes a day are you realistically going to give
   this?
6. **Placement** — A handful of quick design questions so the app can
   tailor the difficulty curve. You can skip if you'd rather start fresh.
7. **Results & Sign-up** — A little summary of your placement and an
   *optional* moment to create an account (Google, email, or skip
   entirely and stay anonymous).

You can absolutely use Grafly without an account. Signing in just means
your progress follows you between phones, and you can recover your streak
if you ever lose your device.

## 6. The Five Tabs at the Bottom

The bottom of the screen is a **floating, frosted-glass pill** with five
icon-only tabs. Each one is a separate world inside the app:

1. **Home** — Your daily landing page. Your streak greets you here, along
   with a beautiful card showing the next lesson to continue, and a
   horizontally scrolling row of premium course cards. This is the only
   place your **streak number** is shown — keeping it special.
2. **Path** *(the Skill Tree)* — A vertical map of every node and module
   you can unlock. More on this in [section 9](#9-the-skill-tree).
3. **Critique** — Where you chat with the AI design mentor about real
   designs. More on this in [section 10](#10-talking-to-the-ai-mentor).
4. **Shop** — Spend the coins you've earned on cosmetics, hearts, streak
   shields, and XP boosters. More in [section 12](#12-the-shop).
5. **Profile** — Your stats, settings, the option to change your name,
   theme, voice on/off, and sign-in.

Some numbers only show up in certain places by design — for example,
your **hearts** appear only inside a lesson, and your **coins** show up
only on the home screen, the shop, and the lesson-results screen. This
keeps each screen visually clean and gives every tab its own personality.

## 7. How a Lesson Actually Works

A Grafly lesson is short. Most take three to seven minutes. They follow
a simple, satisfying rhythm:

1. **Intro card.** Sometimes a lesson opens with one or two soft intro
   screens that set the scene: "today we'll look at hierarchy" — with a
   visual example to anchor the idea.
2. **Mini challenges.** A series of bite-sized interactive questions
   (the [thirteen kinds in the next section](#8-the-thirteen-kinds-of-mini-game)).
   Tap, drag, choose, arrange.
3. **Instant feedback.** Right after each answer, the mascot reacts —
   a celebratory pose if you got it, a gentle "oops" if you didn't —
   and you get a quick explanation of *why* the right answer works.
4. **Hearts and XP.** Right answers earn XP. Wrong answers cost a heart.
   Run out of hearts? You're nudged to refill (free with time, or paid
   with coins, or unlimited if you're Pro).
5. **A way out.** If you tap the close button mid-lesson, Grafly checks
   in: "leave now and lose your progress?" so you never accidentally
   throw away the work you just did.
6. **The reward.** Finish the lesson and a celebration screen appears
   with the XP you earned, your new coin balance, and — if you just
   finished a whole module — a special "module complete" celebration.

The pacing is deliberately quick. Grafly trusts you to come back tomorrow
rather than trying to hold you for an hour today.

## 8. The Thirteen Kinds of Mini Game

Variety is what keeps Grafly from getting stale. Every lesson is built
from one or more of these thirteen interactive question types:

1. **Multiple choice** — Pick the right answer from a few options.
2. **True or false** — Quick gut-check on a design statement.
3. **Identify the element** — Tap the part of the image that matches a
   description (where's the call-to-action? where's the eyebrow?).
4. **Spot the difference** — Two near-identical layouts — find the
   meaningful design change.
5. **Tap the element** — Like identify, but on a real-looking screen.
6. **Arrange in order** — Put steps, hierarchy levels, or sizes into
   the correct sequence.
7. **Drag to match** — Pair labels with the parts of a design they
   describe.
8. **Fill in the blank** — Type the missing word in a design principle.
9. **Spot the bad design** — Pick the version that breaks the rules.
10. **Choose the better design** — Two options, both fine on paper —
    which one is *actually* stronger?
11. **Drag and drop layout** — Build a small layout by snapping pieces
    into a grid.
12. **Five-second test** — A poster flashes for five seconds. Then it's
    gone. Now: what was the headline?
13. **Find the call-to-action** — Tap the button you'd notice first as
    a real user.

Together, these cover everything from typography and hierarchy to
spacing, colour, layout, and gut-level "feel" tests. By rotating
between them, every lesson stays fresh.

## 9. The Skill Tree

The Path tab is a vertical map of your learning journey. Right now the
core curriculum is **Design Principles** — six big modules, each with
three to five lessons:

- Modules unlock as you finish the previous ones, so the difficulty
  ramps gently.
- Each lesson on the tree is shown as a node — tap it to peek at what's
  inside before you commit.
- A "Courses" button opens a fullscreen slide-in **course picker**, so
  in the future when more courses arrive (logo design, type, colour,
  product UX) you'll switch between them here.
- When you complete a whole module, the tree pops a celebration banner
  the next time you open it.

The visual style is editorial: it feels less like a video-game world map
and more like a well-laid-out chapter index in a thoughtful book.

## 10. Talking to the AI Mentor

The Critique tab is where Grafly stops being a quiz and starts being a
conversation.

You're shown a real design — a coffee shop social post, a quote graphic,
a sneaker ad, a travel poster — and you start chatting with **Grafly**,
the in-app design mentor. You can ask things like:

- "What's working in this layout?"
- "Where's my eye supposed to go first?"
- "Would this read well on a small phone?"
- "What would you try if you were redesigning it?"

Grafly is *not* a sterile robot. It's been carefully tuned to feel like
"a friend who happens to be a senior designer: curious, grounded, never
preachy." Specifically:

- It **asks more questions than it answers** — about 80 % of the time
  it'll turn the question back on you, like a great teacher would.
- It **never pretends to edit the image** in front of you — it talks
  about *what it sees* and *what you could try*, not "okay, I just
  moved the headline."
- It **never calls the design "your work"** — because *you* didn't make
  it; you're studying it together.
- It **keeps replies short** — usually four to eight lines so the chat
  feels like texting a mentor, not reading an essay.
- It **replies in your language** — if you write in Arabic, Spanish,
  French, or anything else, it responds in kind.

The chat itself uses a special **star-shaped avatar** for the mentor (the
"AiBot") rather than the octopus mascot — they're deliberately different
characters. The mascot is your cheerleader; the AiBot is your tutor.

When the mentor is "thinking" you'll see a soft spinning animation on
the avatar. When it speaks, the words type themselves out one character
at a time — a small touch that makes the whole exchange feel alive.

If you have your **voice setting** on, the mentor's replies can also be
spoken aloud in a clear, friendly voice.

## 11. The Game Layer (XP, Coins, Hearts, Streak, Shields, Boosters)

This is the part that makes Grafly feel like a game. Six things to
track:

- **XP.** Experience points you earn for every right answer and every
  finished lesson. As your XP grows, you level up. Each level costs
  about 30 % more XP than the last, so the climb feels meaningful but
  never impossible.
- **Coins.** A soft currency you earn by finishing lessons, hitting
  daily goals, and completing achievements. Spend them in the shop.
- **Hearts.** You start each lesson with five. Wrong answers cost one.
  When you run out you have to wait, refill with coins, or upgrade to
  Pro for unlimited hearts. Hearts only appear *inside* a lesson.
- **Streak.** The number of days in a row you've done at least one
  lesson. Streak is shown only on the home screen — it's your morning
  high-five.
- **Streak Shields.** A protective item you can hold in your inventory.
  If you miss a day, a shield is automatically used to keep your
  streak alive — no panic, no guilt.
- **XP Boosters.** A 24-hour multiplier that doubles all the XP you
  earn during the window. Great for binge sessions or long weekends.

There's also a **weekly leaderboard** so you can see how you're doing
versus other learners that week. Friendly, optional, and easy to ignore
if you're more of a quiet learner.

## 12. The Shop

The shop tab is where coins go to live their best life. You can spend
them on:

- **Refilling hearts** so you can keep going without waiting.
- **Streak shields** to protect your streak from a missed day.
- **XP boosters** to double your XP for a day.
- **Mascot cosmetics** — different accessories and outfits for the
  octopus, including the cool yellow and cool purple variants.

Everything in the shop is optional. You can play through the whole
curriculum without ever spending a coin — they just give you a way to
customise the experience and dig yourself out of the occasional jam.

## 13. Going Pro

There's an optional **Pro** subscription that removes the everyday
friction. Pro gives you:

- **Unlimited hearts** — never have to stop a lesson midway because
  you ran out.
- **Unlimited mentor critique** — chat with the AI as much as you want
  per day instead of bumping into a free-tier limit.
- A small **Pro badge** on your profile so the leaderboard knows
  you're committed.

The paywall screen is reached when you tap the Pro upsell on the home
screen, hit the daily critique limit, or run out of hearts mid-lesson
and want unlimited. There's no nagging, no constantly buried free
features — just one clear "if this keeps mattering to you, here's
what unlocks."

## 14. Your Profile and Account

The Profile tab is your personal dashboard:

- **Your stats** — current level, total XP, longest streak, best week.
- **Your name and avatar** — both editable any time, no big deal.
- **Theme toggle** — light or dark. Light is the default; dark is
  gorgeous at night.
- **Voice on/off** — controls whether the mentor speaks its replies.
- **Sign-in / sign-out** — link a Google account or email so your
  progress follows you, or stay fully anonymous.
- **Manage Pro** — see your subscription status if you have one.

Anything stored on your device follows you across the app even if
you've never signed in. The moment you sign in, everything quietly
syncs to the cloud so you can lose your phone and still come back to
the same streak.

## 15. The Octopus Mascot and the Star Mentor

Grafly has *two* characters and they do different jobs:

### The Octopus (the Mascot)

A friendly, expressive octopus that lives all over the app. It has a
small library of moods it cycles through depending on what's happening:

- **Idle** — the default chill pose.
- **Think** — when you're mid-question and the mascot is "considering"
  with you.
- **Correct** — a small celebration when you get something right.
- **Wrong** / **Oops** — a sympathetic shrug when you don't.
- **Celebrate** — for big moments like finishing a module.
- **Cool yellow** and **cool purple** — alternate looks you can unlock.

The mascot is the *only* character allowed to bounce — it has a
deliberate springiness because that's its personality. Everything else
in the app moves with smooth, restrained, professional easing.

### The Star (the AiBot Mentor)

A separate, distinct identity that *only* appears in the Critique chat.
It's the visual voice of the AI tutor: a soft spinning star while
thinking, a calm steady star while speaking. Keeping the mascot and the
mentor visually distinct is intentional — one is your cheerleader, the
other is your teacher, and they shouldn't blur into each other.

## 16. The Brand Reel, the Pitch Deck, and the Design Playground

Beyond the app itself, Grafly ships with three sibling pieces:

### The Brand Reel

A roughly two-minute animated film that walks viewers through Grafly
in cinematic order: the logo emerges, the mascot enters, the tagline
lands, and then a sequence of beautifully animated feature scenes
showcases the gamified tracks, the mini games, the AI mentor, your
progression, the shop, and the leaderboard / Pro tier — before a warm
"thanks for watching" sign-off. It's the kind of thing you'd put on
the app store, a landing page, or in a demo-day video reel.

### The Pitch Deck

A presentation built for investors, hackathon judges, and anyone who
wants the strategic story. It covers:

- The **problem** — why most design education is passive and why that
  fails.
- The **audience** — aspiring designers, students, career-switchers.
- **What's next** — near-term roadmap (more games, Arabic support).
- **Future plans** — bigger dreams (AI tutors, multiplayer modes).
- A warm **closing** with the mascot celebrating and a clear next-step
  call to action.

### The Design Playground

An internal-only sandbox where the team prototypes new components and
compares design variants side by side before deciding which one ships
into the real app. End users never see this — it's the workshop where
the next batch of polish gets shaped.

## 17. Languages, Voice, and Accessibility

A few quiet but important things Grafly cares about:

- **Multilingual mentor** — chat with the AI in your own language and
  it'll reply in kind. Arabic, Spanish, French, and many others all
  work today.
- **Voice option** — turn the mentor's spoken voice on or off in the
  profile settings. The voice itself is a warm, clear speaking voice,
  not a robotic one.
- **High contrast** — colours throughout the app are chosen to meet
  modern accessibility contrast standards in both light and dark
  themes. Text on coloured backgrounds is checked to make sure it stays
  readable, not just decorative.
- **Custom-tuned typography** — the chosen body font was picked for
  comfortable on-screen reading at small sizes.
- **Press feedback** — every important button gives a small, satisfying
  "press" animation so the app always feels responsive.
- **No hyphens or dashes in copy** — a small editorial choice that
  keeps every screen feeling polished instead of clipped.

## 18. Privacy, Sign-In, and Your Stuff Across Devices

You can use Grafly entirely anonymously — no account, no email, just
open and play. Everything you do is saved to your phone.

If you choose to **sign in**, you get two superpowers:

1. Your progress (XP, level, streak, completed lessons, coins,
   shields) syncs quietly to the cloud and follows you to a new phone.
2. If you ever lose your device or reinstall the app, your account
   restores everything.

You can sign in two ways:

- **Continue with Google** — one tap, no password to remember.
- **Email and password** — if you'd rather not use Google.

Behind the scenes, Grafly is careful not to store anything sensitive
on the device that shouldn't be there, and to keep its connection to
the cloud authenticated end to end. Things like microphone access (used
only for voice features) are clearly requested when needed and never
recorded silently.

## 19. The Roadmap (What's Coming Next)

Grafly is built to grow. The pitch deck calls out the near-term and
long-term roadmap explicitly:

**Soon:**
- More mini-game types — every new question format makes the lessons
  feel fresher.
- **Arabic language support** for the full app interface (the AI
  mentor already speaks Arabic).
- More courses beyond Design Principles — typography, colour theory,
  logo design, and product UX are all on the table.

**Later:**
- A more advanced AI tutor that can give personalised, ongoing
  feedback over weeks of practice.
- Multiplayer modes — design battles with friends, daily duels,
  collaborative critiques.
- Deeper customisation of the mascot, the home screen, and the
  learning path itself.

**Always:**
- Polishing the editorial visual identity that makes Grafly feel
  unmistakable in the crowded learning-app space.

## 20. House Rules (How Grafly Talks and Behaves)

A handful of quiet design rules govern how Grafly looks and sounds.
You won't read these in the app, but you'll *feel* them every time you
open it:

1. **Calm, restrained motion.** Animations always settle smoothly —
   they never bounce or overshoot. The single exception is the
   octopus mascot, which bounces on purpose because that's its
   personality.
2. **No hyphens or dashes in any UI copy.** Sentences breathe with
   commas, periods, and white space instead.
3. **Stats live where they belong.** Your streak only shows on home,
   your hearts only inside a lesson, and your coins only on home, in
   the shop, and on the lesson-results screen. Each tab keeps its
   own visual identity instead of being bombarded with numbers.
4. **The mascot is sacred.** It only ever appears through one
   approved component — never copy-pasted, never reskinned in random
   spots — so its personality stays consistent.
5. **Light is the default theme.** Dark is fully supported and
   gorgeous, but Grafly opens light because that matches the
   editorial, paper-like feel.
6. **Editorial design always wins ties.** Magazine-style headers,
   confident type, generous spacing, near-black pill primary buttons,
   subtle background motifs — when in doubt, do the editorial thing.
7. **Every primary button has a press animation.** No exceptions —
   it's the small detail that makes the app feel alive.
8. **Iterative development.** Big changes are discussed before they
   ship. Nothing major lands by surprise.
9. **The mascot and the AI mentor are different characters.** The
   octopus is your cheerleader; the star is your teacher. They never
   blend.
10. **Words on screen go through one approved text style.** That way,
    when the team decides to refine the typography, every screen
    follows along automatically.
